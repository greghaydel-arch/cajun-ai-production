Setting Up a Historical Trading Journal

A practical structure for documenting completed trades and separating observed facts from later interpretation.

By Cajun AI Editorial·Updated October 22, 2026·Educational guide

Editorial update: October 22, 2026. Educational content; not a product benchmark or professional advice.

A journal can organize a review process without promising better returns. Keep factual records separate from your interpretation so that later notes do not silently change the underlying history.

A useful entry structure

  • Reference: identify the owned account and completed execution or calculation being reviewed.
  • Observed facts: retain timestamps, quantities, prices and known fees from the source record.
  • Reflection: describe what you remember, what the records establish and what remains uncertain.
  • Review question: write a retrospective question you can investigate without treating one outcome as proof of a strategy.

Protect the record

Avoid storing broker credentials or unnecessary personal information in notes. If two tabs edit an entry, resolve the conflict explicitly. Do not assume an unsaved draft or a failed save has reached the server.
