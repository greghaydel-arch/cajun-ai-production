Reviewing Historical Trading Performance

Check the source records, fees and calculation scope before interpreting historical trading results.

By Cajun AI Editorial·Updated October 22, 2026·Educational guide

Editorial update: October 22, 2026. Educational content; not a product benchmark or professional advice.

A retrospective review begins with reliable records. Preserve the original export and distinguish executions, reconstructed trades and account-level cash movements. Different grouping rules can produce different trade counts.

Check the calculation inputs

  • Confirm the account, currency, date range and timestamp convention.
  • Look for duplicate or missing fills, and distinguish a missing fee from a known zero fee.
  • Record the matching method, such as FIFO, and whether unmatched inventory remains.
  • Check when the calculation was last run; newly imported history may not yet be included.

Interpret summaries with their limits

Win rate depends on how a “trade” and a profitable outcome are defined. Profit factor divides gross gains by the magnitude of gross losses; when gross losses are zero, that ratio is not a normal finite result. Neither metric has a universal threshold that proves a durable advantage. Fees, sample selection and the observation period matter.

This is educational record review, not an investment recommendation, tax calculation or prediction.
