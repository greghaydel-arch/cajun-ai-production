Trailing Stops: Trigger Prices Are Not Guaranteed Fills

An educational explanation of trailing-stop mechanics, execution uncertainty and retrospective record review.

By Cajun AI Editorial·Updated October 22, 2026·Educational guide

Editorial update: October 22, 2026. Educational content; not a product benchmark or professional advice.

A trailing stop adjusts its trigger level by an amount or percentage relative to a moving reference price. The broker’s exact trigger convention and supported order type matter. This explanation is not a recommendation to place an order or choose a particular distance.

A hypothetical calculation

For a hypothetical sell trailing stop at 5% below a $62 reference high, the calculated trigger is $58.90. A subsequent price of $59 is still above that trigger. The earlier example on this page incorrectly said that a fall to $59 would trigger it.

A trigger is not an exit guarantee

Investor.gov explains that a stop order becomes a market order when triggered, and market orders do not guarantee an execution price. A gap or fast market can therefore produce a fill different from the stop level. A limit condition instead introduces the possibility that an order will not execute. Do not describe a trailing stop as automatically “locking in” a specific gain.

Review what actually happened

For historical analysis, distinguish the intended trigger, broker-reported order events and actual fills. An execution-only CSV may not contain the full order lifecycle. TradeBoard does not place orders, manage stops or reconstruct unavailable order context. Its invited scope remains historical analytics, journaling and education.
